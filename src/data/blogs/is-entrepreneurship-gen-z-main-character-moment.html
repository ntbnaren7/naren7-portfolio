Pretty funny thing to talk about when you're literally one among them, and you've already been on the journey (I still am, but not in the traditional way anymore - because that's changed). Uhmm, I don't want this to be another generic blog. When this question came up in my feed, I simply wanted to talk about it because I can relate to it well.

Entrepreneurship isn't the trend. Ownership is.

When I first stepped into this, I was very excited. I've talked to founders, VCs, CEOs, and a lot of entrepreneurial people. One thing that I didn't want to happen was this: entrepreneurship should not become another trend, like how YouTube became. See, it's not wrong, but most people fail because they want to become entrepreneurs only because they find it cool to become one and have their own "What color is your Bugatti?" moment in life.

But what I'm seeing now is that entrepreneurship has shifted to ownership in Gen Z's minds. Meaning, people just want ownership - and that's exactly what we're seeing today in the form of:

  • Startups
  • SaaS
  • Newsletters
  • YouTube
  • Agencies
  • Open source sponsorship
  • Investing
  • Creator businesses

Yes, even I'm among them, as I said earlier. And there are many out there without even realizing this is the truth - it's that wanting to own the upside.

AI and entrepreneurs

See, AI didn't create entrepreneurs, nor is it creating entrepreneurs - but it has, for sure, removed the excuses. People before the age of AI needed:

  • A designer → today we have ChatGPT and numerous AI design tools.
  • An engineer → today we have Claude Code, Cursor, Antigravity, and many more.
  • Marketing → we have tons of image and video creation tools like Midjourney, Veo, Blaze, Scribe.. and what not, bro?

The previous limiting factor was skills, which has now shifted to taste, judgment, and execution.

The real bottleneck isn't building anymore

This has to be the biggest shift happening right now. Just like how, a few years back, the internet was flooded with "AI is taking away jobs," and that got answered with "AI won't. People using AI will." Now everyone says AI builds products, but only a few people realize that everyone can now build. So building becomes a commodity.

The scarce resources become:

  • Trust
  • Credibility
  • Customer understanding
  • Verification
  • Validation
  • Distribution
  • Speed of learning

A founder in 2030 will spend very little time coding and a lot more time understanding customers.

Gen Z is becoming less career-oriented and more portfolio-oriented

This is true. You're literally seeing that right now - even in this blog, which is me doing exactly that. The days of:

Job → Promotion → Retirement

have shifted towards:

Internship → Open Source → Blog → Portfolio → Personal Brand → Freelance → SaaS → Angel Investing → Another Startup

If you actually manage to pull this off, then your work life = absolute cinema. Because this is how careers are being built - or at least, how they've started shifting. (Also, I'm not saying this is the only way possible. I'm saying this has become a serious shift.)

Entrepreneurship isn't replacing jobs

It's replacing waiting. Yep. Previous generations had to wait for several things:

  • Permission
  • Promotions
  • Funding
  • Experience

..and a lot more that I can keep listing. Gen Z is much more likely to ship something first and then learn in public. Of course, it's got its own advantages and serious cons as well. But waiting is no longer the problem. People can literally ship one product or even one feature and call it a day. That's one serious and very real behavioral change.

The biggest challenge isn't failure

It's distraction. I'm struggling with it too. Today's founders have access to X, LinkedIn, Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, AI news, and 500 new tools every month. Everyone wants to build - agents, MCPs, RAGs, voice AI, and whatnot. The challenge has shifted from a lack of opportunities to choosing one long enough to become excellent at it.

Social media created a distorted definition of entrepreneurship

See, from what I've seen (I travel a lot and meet a lot of people - students, founders, passionate builders), many think entrepreneurship is just about:

  • Applying to YC
  • Raising money
  • Making millions
  • Getting a private jet

That's what they think it is. But nah. Even your local dentist running three clinics is an entrepreneur. One serious concern is that Gen Z often confuses visibility with success.

Entrepreneurship takes serious effort. A lot of sacrifice. A lot of time. It also means you'll be alone. You'll lose a lot. It'll be hard. But it also takes genuine mind and heart to overcome all of that and become one.

Why am I saying this? Because every day, Gen Z sees:

"19-year-old makes $4M MRR."
"Built a SaaS in 2 weeks."
"Quit college."
"College dropout built this, built that, raised millions."

These may be true. But they're also influencing people's career aspirations. Social media alone plays a vital role in career damage - it builds anxiety.

The biggest opportunity for Gen Z isn't AI

It's domain expertise. See, every one of us has access to the same models. No excuses. Doesn't matter if you're on a free plan and your friend is on a premium Pro plan. You both have something to start with.

But only a very few understand. AI amplifies expertise. It doesn't replace it. It never can or will. The next billion-dollar companies may come from people who deeply understand a specific industry and use AI to transform it - and it's already happened: Cursor.

Anyone can access the same frontier models. But only a few:

  • Understand customers better than anyone else
  • Integrate into existing workflows
  • Know industry regulations
  • Earn user trust
  • Collect proprietary data over time

Be that few. Because this is the moat now. This is exactly why someone with ten years in healthcare may build a stronger healthcare AI company than someone with even 100 years in AI but no healthcare experience. This is also why hackathons and many industry-level competitions still revolve around the same sectors - healthcare, defense, finance.

Technology is increasingly commoditized. But domain knowledge? Nah.

Final thoughts

Maybe the biggest shift isn't that Gen Z wants to become entrepreneurs. Maybe it's that we no longer want to wait for permission to create. And in a world where anyone can build, the winners won't be those with the best AI. They'll be those who understand people and problems the best.
